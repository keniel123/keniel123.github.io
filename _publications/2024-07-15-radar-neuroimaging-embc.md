Conventional neuroimaging (MRI, CT) is powerful but expensive, immobile, and not always accessible. This paper explores whether **radar technology can serve as a low-cost, non-invasive complement for brain imaging and tumour detection**.

We simulated electromagnetic interactions within anatomical brain-tissue models using Ansys HFSS, evaluating signal penetration, signal strength, and safety constraints across Patch and Vivaldi antenna configurations. The results characterise the trade-offs between the two: Patch antennas proved better suited to tumour localisation, while Vivaldi antennas favoured broader scanning coverage.

**Recognition:** presented as an oral talk at IEEE EMBC 2024 in Orlando, FL, where the work received the **IEEE EMBC NextGen Scholar Award**.
