I'm a PhD researcher in Artificial Intelligence at the **University of Southampton**, part of the UKRI MINDS Centre for Doctoral Training. I work at the intersection of **computer vision, 3D human motion modelling, and radar sensing** — building AI that understands how people move, even when a camera can't see them.

The thread running through my research is *human sensing, end to end*: I build the datasets, the models, the simulators, and the evaluation pipelines — and I care that results hold up on real hardware, not just on benchmarks.

**Research interests:** 3D human mesh recovery · radar & RF sensing · differentiable simulation · foundation-model transfer · multimodal perception

What I work on
======

* **3D humans from video.** Temporally coherent whole-body mesh recovery — body, hands, and face together — from ordinary monocular video, built on large vision transformers and evaluated across 12 datasets (~12 million samples).
* **Differentiable radar simulation.** Rendering physically faithful radar micro-Doppler straight from animated 3D human meshes, so synthetic data can stand in for expensive radar captures.
* **Foundation models beyond vision.** How far do frozen DINOv2, CLIP, and MAE features transfer to radar and WiFi spectrograms? Further than you'd think — cross-sensor recognition turns out to be a representation-transfer problem.
* **Radar-based neuroimaging.** Feasibility of radar for non-invasive brain imaging and tumour detection — published at [IEEE EMBC 2024](/publication/2024-embc-radar-neuroimaging) (oral presentation, NextGen Scholar Award).

Several first-author manuscripts from this work are currently under review at international venues — the [research page](/research/) has the high-level story of each.
